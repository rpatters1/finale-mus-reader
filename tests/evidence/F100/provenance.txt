Finale 1.0.0 for Macintosh, Coda-banner era, big-endian.

Origin: controlled test documents created by Robert G. Patterson in Finale
1.0.0 running under Mac OS 9 hosted by SheepShaver. The documents and their
musical content are released by their author under this repository's MIT
license.

Every changed file below was derived from the same newly created baseline, and
each alters one named control or a small named set of them. Pairwise comparison
among the changed files localizes the intended changes to the named physical
tuples; the baseline has a smaller save allocation and is therefore a semantic
rather than byte-for-byte control for those files.

F100-clef-baseline.mus
    sha256 56cbf72c98e535732319fdb4bcfa73ebabd48eea6933df924ec5a9a4b79da641
    4,655 bytes. Changes the Clef Designer baseline adjustment on two clefs and
    checks the box governing whether the adjustments are used. Selector `28(65534)`
    word 4 moves -2 -> -4 and `29(65534)` word 4 moves -4 -> 2; no other record word
    in the file changes, so the checkbox leaves no trace in the others or details
    pools. The values are harmonic levels. Finale 27 discards them: the companion
    emits no `<baseAdjust>` at all.

F100-stemopts-changed.mus
    sha256 3800ba1964aaa754f1aa2223b79450885e4078a428b2c996fe3de6842db03330
    4,655 bytes. Lengthens the normal and shortened stems by one staff position
    each and switches off "Display Reverse Stemming". Selector `20(65534)` word 4
    moves 7 -> 8 and word 5 moves 5 -> 6, and `41(65534)` word 1 moves 0 -> 1.
    Its companion moves the two lengths to 96 and 72 and gains
    `<noReverseStems/>`, which is what makes the pre-Finale-3.5 lengths a
    measured staff-position count rather than an inference, and what shows this
    era keeps the reverse-stemming flag in bit 0 where later ones use bit 2.
    `21(65534)` word 0 also moves 4 -> 5: the Finale 1.0.0 dialog offers "Offset"
    beside the two lengths, and that word is the augmentation-dot upstem-flag
    adjustment from Finale 3.0 on. Finale 27 discards it when converting a Coda
    document, so the companion cannot say whether it means the same thing here.
    It is not `revStemAdj`, which is word 2 of the same selector and does not
    move. Two further rows differ, `54(65534)` and `55(65534)`.

F100-revstem-25.mus
    sha256 19ced7ea65905d118a665f63ccc30061546a3b8fed41545bdf8c2cb39a13adee
    4,143 bytes. Sets the reverse stem adjustment to 25. Selector `21(65534)`
    word 2 moves 18 -> 25 and the companion carries `<revStemAdj>300</revStemAdj>`,
    which is twelve times the stored number and the same factor the two stem
    lengths establish. That makes every pre-Finale-3.5 length a measured
    staff-position count.
    This save has the baseline's smaller save allocation, so unlike the other
    controlled files it also differs in font, text and layout rows that no edit
    touched. Those are allocation noise; the ETF confirms the intended word
    independently.

F100-dateform-tab.mus
    sha256 9991a6d271b21dc000c63628458f813379c928d3b149e7c868a6b18c000cb226
    From F100-baseline, the date format set to Long and the tab stop to 7 spaces.
    Selector 05 word 5 moves 0 -> 1 and selector 13 word 0 moves 4 -> 7; the companion
    reads <dateFormat>long</dateFormat> and <tabSpaces>7</tabSpaces>. With its Finale 2.6.3
    counterpart this is the evidence that the two oldest text options occupy the same words
    in the earliest era as in the latest, so neither needs a version gate. The era's UI
    exposes no other document-wide text setting, and no Coda-banner document carries the
    accidental-insert block at all.
