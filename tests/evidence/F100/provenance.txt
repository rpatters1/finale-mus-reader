Finale 1.0.0 for Macintosh, Coda-banner era, big-endian.

Origin: controlled test documents created by Robert G. Patterson in Finale
1.0.0 running under Mac OS 9 hosted by SheepShaver. The documents and their
musical content are released by their author under this repository's MIT
license.

Every changed file below was derived from the same newly created baseline, and
each alters one named control or a small named set of them. Pairwise comparison
among the changed files localizes the intended changes to the named physical
tuples; the baseline has a smaller save allocation and is therefore a semantic
rather than byte-for-byte control for those files.

Text recovery: four documents that opened the era's own text framing

    This era stores text two ways and neither is the text pool of the later ones.
    Block text is in the `HT` and `HS` others families, one pair of records per
    block: `HT` holds the characters in four consecutive incidences and `HS` holds
    the style, keyed so that `HS` incidence n describes the nth `HT` record of the
    same comparator. Lyric text is in the region behind the last record pool, as
    two length-prefixed chunks whose second carries spelled-out `^verse(n)`,
    `^chorus(n)` and `^section(n)` records.

    Before these four, no Coda-banner document in any survey contained text of any
    kind. Twenty-six were tracked and every one was silent, so the era looked like
    a framing problem when it was in fact an evidence problem.

    One controlled pair, and the reason the framing could be read at all. Both hold
    one block text; the second lengthens it from `short` to
    `longer with <page insert> page insert`. Only two records move between them,
    `HT` and `HS`, which is what says the block is those two and nothing else.

    The `HT` record is a fixed four incidences either way, so the shorter save
    leaves the previous one's bytes after the terminator. Reading to the first NUL
    rather than to the end of the record is what that shows to be necessary.

    `HS` word 4 moves from 0 to 2, which is exactly the `^page(2)` the companion
    writes, and that is what identifies the word as the insert's argument.

    This pair also confirms the Coda TextBlock synthesis. There is no `TX` record
    in either MUS or ETF. The ordered `HS`/`HT` pair produces TextBlock 1 pointing
    to BlockText 1; `HS` word 5 low bits select left justification, and the Finale
    27 companion agrees on the complete resulting layout.

    sha256 26c0b72bec4578d645fa2a2afe71cb55ae2d927eafdfb51b9d214505beb23fb4
    The only document of the era anywhere with lyrics, and the one that shows the
    text region is used after all. Its `^lyric` chunk carries a verse, a chorus and
    a section, each in a different font and two of them with an effect run, where
    the `^text` chunk beside it is empty even in the block-text fixtures above.

    Its effect runs are written spaced apart -- `^efx(plain) ^efx(bold)` -- which a
    reader that closed a run at the first non-command byte would split into one
    `^nfx` per half. Both are one statement of one style, and the spaces are
    literal text belonging before the single command. This is the only document
    that states the case.
